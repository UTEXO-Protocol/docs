USDT on Bitcoin is issued and transferred with the [RGB protocol](/getting-started/rgb-concepts). Supporting it touches two sides of a swap integration: the **platform** that holds and moves the user's RGB USDT, and the **resolver** that settles the Bitcoin leg of the swap.

</Warning>

## Platform side: add RGB USDT

A platform (exchange, wallet, or swap provider) needs to hold RGB assets, receive them with RGB invoices, and send them. All of this runs on one [RGB Lightning Node (RLN)](/overview):

1. **Learn the model.** RGB state lives off-chain and is validated client-side; Bitcoin UTXOs anchor it. Read [Getting Started with RGB](/getting-started/rgb-concepts) first — invoices, colored UTXOs, and consignments behave differently from account-based chains.
2. **Run a node.** Follow the [RLN Quick Start](/rgb-lightning-node/quickstart) to launch a node, fund it, prepare colored UTXOs, and receive and send RGB USDT on-chain.
3. **Choose your deployment.** [Self-host RLN](/rgb-lightning-node/self-hosted-rgb-lightning-node) or run it on [Utexo Cloud](/product-suite/RLN-overview).
4. **Keep keys where you need them.** If your custody model requires keys outside the node, see [Remote Signer](/security/rln-remote-signer).

On mainnet, RLN supports on-chain RGB operations. Lightning is available on testnet only; the same RLN integration picks up Lightning when it reaches mainnet, with no second integration.

## Resolver side: settle the Bitcoin leg

Resolvers settle Bitcoin swaps with a Taproot HTLC. See [Bitcoin Settlement](/product-suite/swap/resolver-integration/bitcoin-settlement) for the HTLC lock output, deposit transaction, spend paths, and fee model, and [Resolver Overview](/product-suite/swap/resolver-integration/overview) for the full lifecycle.
